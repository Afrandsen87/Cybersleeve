# Vision: Story, tone and the sixth action

Part of the long-term design target; index and status markers in `docs/VISION.md`.

## Fantasy and story

- The player is a down-on-their-luck nobody who scrapes together just enough money for **Sleeve treatment**: an experimental process that decouples consciousness from the physical body (the **sleeve**). It gives fine-grained control over the brain's processing power and a direct interface to digital systems. That is what lets the player start hacking.
- Wealth grows until the player can afford **re-sleeving**: the body is old, they want a new one. Over time the mind adapts to its new reality (**sleeve XP**), making the player better at every task, so each life progresses faster.
- **Pacing intent.** Hour 1 feels slow but with meaningful progress towards visible goals. Slow stretches are used to **drip-feed story and mechanics**, leaving time to read, test and internalise. The second run-through is much faster, with the story adapting to the player's re-sleeve status and unlocking new upgrades and mechanics. Target: the first arrival at corporations is **days of play**, not hours (today it can be reached much earlier; pacing will be adjusted later).

## Story, tone and delivery

- **Origin.** A corporation invented the Sleeve procedure but could not legally test it, so it sold the treatment cheap enough that someone desperate would take it despite explicit warnings. The player takes the chance, ends up **buying the research team** and improving the technology until re-sleeving is real. Eventually the player becomes too powerful and **the universe needs to reset** (ties into the story resets and the singularity; how exactly is OPEN).
- **Tone/theme.** A mix of Cyberpunk 2077 and Altered Carbon: neon-punk noir hybrid. The current colour scheme is close enough; no restyle planned.
- **Delivery.** Every upgrade has **lore text** explaining contextually what it does. Unlocking an action shows a **poster message** explaining what the action does and what it represents. Modal story popups are possible but **to be avoided**. Reading should be needed to get the game fully, but a player who skips can still work out how to play.
- **No concrete NPCs.** Lore pieces use quotes, comments and name references, as in found journal entries, e.g. *"Reeve told me this upgrade might fry my brain, but it also comes with enough ICE to rival a small army. I'm just going to go for it. Reeve can have my quadbike if I don't make it." (journal entry, blood and interface paste all over the page)*.
- Slow stretches of the first life are where story and mechanics are drip-fed (see 1).

## The sixth sleeve action

Re-sleeving unlocks new upgrades that carry a specific **tag**. The sixth (final) sleeve action is a **broad boost to all of those effects**, representing the player unlocking the sleeve's full capability now that they have no attachment to it as a physical body: cybernetic augments everywhere, specialised interface limbs, unnecessary organs removed. It should feel like a **big, meaningful boost on the way to the corporation stage**. (Corporations unlock early in a run but really take off later.)
